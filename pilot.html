The shape

The next ones build on it.

The pilot stands on its own: one workflow, a working system and a measured result. If you go further, the next job doesn't start from zero. The rules, the setup and the know-how are already in place.

A narrow, fixed-price pilot with a gate you control

One job, found together or brought by you. We rebuild it around AI on your own seats and your own rules, run it alongside the current process, and measure. The pilot ends with a working system and a number.

Week 1

Scope and the test

We pick the workflow, or find it together if you arrive without one, write down how it works today, and agree the measure that will decide whether the pilot passed. Before anything is built.

The test, in writing
Weeks 2 to 3

Build on your seats

The system is built on the tools your business already pays for, with your rules written into it. Nothing lives on our side.

Your rules file, on your seats
Weeks 3 to 5

Run it beside the old way

The new process shadows the current one. We compare, find the gaps, tighten the rules. Your most experienced person is the judge.

A side-by-side log
Week 6

Handover

One-on-one time with the people who will run it, the rules handed over in plain language, and a short note on what to try next. We stay on call after handover while the new way beds in; that support is part of the pilot, not an extra.

What fits

Good first pilots look alike.

They come back every week, they eat hours, and someone wants them fixed. Check yours against the list below. If it reads more like the second list, it still wants AI, just through your people rather than a pilot.

A good first pilot
  • Happens every week, the same way, and eats hours
  • Needs judgment, not just typing, and the judgment can be written down
  • Has a before: a time, an error rate, a backlog you can measure
  • Runs on documents and data you can share, or can stand in for
  • Is owned by someone who wants it fixed
Better left to people, with AI in hand
  • Happens once, or differently every time
  • Has no measure anyone would agree on
  • Hinges on a relationship, not a process
  • Depends on data nobody is allowed to move
  • Is really a people problem, and people can be trained
